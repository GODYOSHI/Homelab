# Stage 1: Planning my first homelab

## Goal

I wanted a computer I could experiment with instead of using my main machine for every lab. My goals were to learn Linux, get more comfortable with computer hardware and networking, run class labs, and build projects I could document on GitHub. I was also trying to keep the cost down, so I researched used systems before buying anything.

## What I compared

I looked at local Facebook Marketplace listings and eBay. Some options were older Dell desktops, OptiPlex 5040 systems, a late-2014 Mac mini, and eventually newer OptiPlex 7060 and 7070 listings. Smaller machines appealed to me because I did not want a huge setup. I checked the processor, RAM, storage, whether a drive and power cable were included, and the total price after shipping and tax.

A cheap computer with no drive was not automatically a bad option, but it meant another purchase and an OS installation. I also learned that a machine with an older processor or only a hard drive might not give me the experience I wanted if I tried to run several labs.

## Decision at the time

I first chose a used Dell desktop advertised with an Intel i5-6500 and 16 GB of DDR4 RAM but no drive, for about $120. The memory looked useful for a lab, and I expected I could add storage. This was a reasonable starting idea on paper, but I had not yet proved that the hardware worked.
